Why I Started a Bakery Blog

This isn’t so much about finding the best bakery as it is about having a reason to travel, meet people, and see the humanity in their work.

August 21, 2026 • 3 minute read

I think in this modern day, everything is about maximization and optimization. We are so trapped in the perpetual cycle of just do and do, sometimes to the point where we start to lose some of our humanity.

We go to big businesses and consume ready-to-eat products. We watch the news in digestible, ready-to-eat segments. We avoid narratives that disagree with ours. We order our 500% marked-up burger off a tablet, being cooked by an understaffed minimum-wage worker.

At least, I felt that way.

I’ve worked at a pizzeria my whole life. It’s my own family’s, and when I was young, I hated the job. I hated serving people. Everyone was such a prick. Some people would threaten employees and insult them for any reason at all. For the longest time, I hated social interaction. I hated my job. I hated the people.

Everyone is so cruel to one another. Most people do not show the bare minimum to one another. When you go outside, all you see is fighting. When you look at the news, all you see is violence.

However, against the popular narrative that there is no good in the world, I think it is only when you embrace the harshness and cruelty within the world that you can see its innate beauty.

It is only through exploration and visitation of the world that I have met some of the greatest people.

I’ve met people who have given me food when they are in times of need. I have met people who are braver than I could ever imagine, who have the strength to do anything they set their mind to. I have met people who see hardships that could bring anyone to their knees, and in spite of everything, they remain some of the kindest people I’ve ever met.

This isn’t so much about finding the best bakery as it is about having a reason to travel, to meet people, and to see the art and passion people choose to put into their work. To see the humanity as much as I can.

I know it’s far from a common answer, but it’s my true and honest reason.

In Spanish, there is a word: vago. It has two meanings: a homeless person, a vagrant, but it also has another meaning: a man who is never home. My mother once called me a vago, but I didn’t see it as an insult, because to me, she was right in one aspect.

I love seeing the world. And it is my home.
